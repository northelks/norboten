Why join

Four things that are better with other people

When a lab will not pass

You read the check's output, you fixed the symptom, and it still fails — because the cause is elsewhere. Say what you see: the unit, the log line, the output that surprised you. Somebody has been in front of that exact machine.

After you pass

There is rarely one fix. Compare yours with the journal's and with other people's: the interesting part is why one survives a reboot and another does not. This is where most of the learning happens.

What lands next

New labs, journals and question banks are announced as they merge, and so is every release. Ask for the lab you want: the list of what to build next comes from these channels more than from anywhere else.

The person who wrote it

A check that passes when it should not, a briefing that misleads, a fault that is unfair — tell me and it gets fixed, often the same day. I am in the server, not behind a support form.

The bot

It posts, and it writes to you

Two jobs, and it never reads your messages:

  • It announces. Every lab, journal and question bank the day it lands on main, and every release with its notes. Straight from the push, so the same thing is never announced twice and a quiet week stays quiet.
  • It sends your week. Link Discord on the account page and switch the digest on — it is off until you do — and on Sunday evening the bot sends you a direct message: what you attempted, what your ratings did, and the topic worth working on next. No model writes it, so no number in it is invented. A week with nothing in it gets no message.

Everything the link stores is in the privacy policy, and unlinking takes one click. The tutor and the consultant, which do answer questions, live in the terminal and on this site — not in the server.

House rules

Three of them

  1. No solutions to a lab before others have had a go. Symptoms, commands you tried and what surprised you: all welcome. A finished fix, posted the day the lab lands, takes the lab away from everyone else. Put it behind a spoiler tag if you must.
  2. Rated labs are never discussed. Their faults, criteria and answers stay out of the server entirely — that is what makes a rating worth something.
  3. Be the person you needed when you were stuck. Nobody arrives knowing systemd. Answers that explain the reasoning beat answers that paste a command.
